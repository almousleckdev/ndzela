# ADR-0033: Three unpaid days suspend the driver in NDZela and in Zella; paying everything restores both

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-11 · **Amends:** [payments-and-arrears.md](../business/payments-and-arrears.md) ("only an administrator reactivates"), [ADR-0026](0026-charging-accounts-linked-to-drivers.md), [ADR-0028](0028-public-sessions-from-the-zella-feed.md) · **Design:** [spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-11-arrears-suspension-both-apps-design.md)

**Context.** A driver suspended for three unpaid days could keep charging by scanning with another person's Zella account. Nothing between the charger and the OCPP platform identifies the car (the platform knows only the idTag and the chargers cannot send a VIN), so the car cannot be the key. The person can: the Zella account, its phone number and the mobile-money number that pays for it.

**Decision.**
1. At **3 or more unpaid days** the driver is suspended in NDZela (unchanged) and their **linked** Zella account is suspended too. NDZela is the source of truth: it tells Zella through `ZellaCommand` rows (migration V52) that are retried with backoff (1, 2, 4, 8, then 15 minutes) until Zella accepts them, and a reconciler compares each linked driver with what Zella has been told. A suspend order cancels a pending restore and the other way round. A driver with no linked Zella customer is suspended in NDZela only.
2. A driver suspended for arrears has `suspension_cause = ARREARS`; a staff suspension has `MANUAL` (migration V51). **When the driver's unpaid days reach zero** (payment or waiver) the next arrears run restores the driver (audited `DRIVER_REACTIVATED` by `system`) and Zella. A `MANUAL` suspension or a block is never lifted by a payment. A partial payment changes nothing.
3. If a payment is reversed after the automatic restore (`auto_restored_at` set) and three days are unpaid again, the driver is suspended again in both apps. A driver whom staff reactivated by hand is not suspended again by the job.
4. Zella refuses charges and top-ups from a suspended customer on every request, and refuses top-ups paid from a suspended driver's number. Each refusal is recorded as evidence. NDZela reads the evidence through `GET /v1/partners/ndzela/evidence`: it sees the suspended driver in full and the other account only as an irreversible reference, plus its customer id when that account is also a linked NDZela driver ([ADR-0028](0028-public-sessions-from-the-zella-feed.md) still holds: NDZela never learns who ordinary Zella customers are). Zella's own administrators see the receiving account in full.
5. Overlapping sessions, a battery level that cannot belong to one car, and energy far above the driver's usual only raise a case for staff. They never notify or sanction by themselves.
6. The driver is told by e-mail and, in Zella, by push; a helper who is an NDZela driver is warned too, without being told who was helped.

**Consequences.** (+) The shared-account loophole closes as far as the data allows, and nobody has to remember to lift a suspension. (-) The car is still not identified: a friend with their own number can still charge for a suspended driver and only a money trail or the case score will betray it. Charging elsewhere, or at home, is out of reach. The remaining lever is the car itself, as the contract provides.
